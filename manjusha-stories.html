Manjusha Stories

Turning a 7th-century folk art from Bihar into a brand, a product range and a story-led online store.

01 / 06

A story, sold as a souvenir.

Problem statementOnline, buyers can't see the story behind Manjusha products, so a meaningful folk art gets sold as a cheap souvenir.

Every Manjusha painting tells one story: Bihula, who braves the snake goddess to save her husband.

  • Folk art from Bhagalpur, Bihar, with a GI tag since 2021.
  • Mostly seen as wall paintings, festival boxes or souvenirs.
  • Online, it's a grid of products. The meaning disappears.
How might weturn a ritual folk art into everyday objects, and sell them online, without stripping away their meaning?

What I found: 4 visual rules

Manjusha follows four strict rules, and each became a brand rule.

01

Bold line

Strong outlines, simplified characters.

→ A clear, line-based graphic style

02

Symbolic motifs

A visual dictionary: snake, lotus, fish, bird, boat.

→ A motif library for products

03

Restricted palette

A disciplined use of a few colours.

→ Four brand colours from the paintings

04

Narrative composition

Scenes in sequence, framed by borders.

→ Story-led page sections

A palette from pigment.

Ideas I explored, and why

DroppedSell Manjusha wall art

Why I dropped itAlready crowded, and it keeps the art on the wall instead of in daily life.

DroppedCopy motifs straight onto products

Why I dropped itLooks like souvenir merchandise and loses the art's rules.

DroppedShop by product category

Why I dropped itPeople shop for a need ("a gift", "for my desk"), not "trays".

ChosenEveryday objects + story-led store

Why I chose itMotif → abstraction → object keeps the meaning, and shopping by purpose matches how people buy.

What I'll measure
  • Task success rate
  • Time on task
  • Can they retell the story? (comprehension)
  • SUS ease-of-use score
Who & how

5 gift buyers aged 22–35, 20-minute remote sessions with the clickable prototype, thinking aloud.

Goal: check that buyers understand the story and can buy without help.

What I learned

Translating a culture means understanding its rules. Once I knew them, every decision had a reason.

The psychology

People value objects more when they know their story. The motif next to the price builds connection.
