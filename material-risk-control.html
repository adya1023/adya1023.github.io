Material Risk Control

A decision tool that helps plant managers stop material shortages before they stop production.

AI recommendsExpedite PO #45821Protects 3 production orders.

Role
UX research & product design
Type
Concept · Enterprise SaaS
User
Plant materials manager
Tools
Figma, Figma Make, Keynote

Problem

Plant managers spot material shortages too late, because they check 6 signals by hand across different systems.

Solution

A SKU-level decision workflow: see the risk, why it matters and the AI's suggested action, then a human approves.

Outcome

11 manual steps → 1 loop across 7 connected screens, with explainable AI, an audit trail and KPIs to measure it.

2024

2026

04 / 06

From evidence to priorities.

I followed the Double Diamond: steps 02–03 above, then prioritised and designed below.

Ideas I explored, and why

DroppedA classic analytics dashboard

Why I dropped itShows lots of data but doesn't say what to do next.

DroppedFully automatic AI ordering

Why I dropped itManagers lose control and accountability. Low trust.

DroppedEmail alerts only

Why I dropped itNo context, no comparison of options, easy to miss.

ChosenSKU-level decision workflow

Why I chose itRanked risks, the reason, AI options, and a human approve button. Fast and trusted.

MoSCoW: saying no kept it focused

MUST

  • SKU risk + timing
  • Production impact
  • Action options
  • Human approval

SHOULD

  • AI recommendation
  • Confidence / evidence
  • Decision history

COULD

  • Scenario comparison
  • Deeper analytics

WON'T

  • ERP replacement
  • Autonomous buying

User flow I designed

Overview→Open riskiest SKU→Why it's at risk→Affected orders→Approve, modify or reject→Actions tracker→Resolved + reports

Dark = start and end · dashed = decision point

AI + human principleAI does the joining up. People decide. Every suggestion can be approved, modified or rejected, and is logged.

Usability test plan Planned

Tasks
  1. Find the riskiest material and explain why it's at risk.
  2. Find which production orders it affects.
  3. Choose and approve an action.
What I'll measure
  • Time to decision vs a spreadsheet
  • Correct decision rate
  • Trust in the AI suggestion (1–5)
  • SUS ease-of-use score
Who & how

5 participants with operations or supply-chain background (or classmates as stand-ins), same task with a spreadsheet and with my prototype.

Goal: check that managers decide faster and trust the AI's reasons.

How I'd measure success after launch

Business KPI

From opening the tool to choosing an action.

Trust

Accept rate

AI suggestions accepted, modified or rejected.

What I learned

Good UX in complex tools removes the decisions a user shouldn't have to make.

The psychology

Under pressure people can't juggle many signals. One ranked, explained risk lowers cognitive load.
